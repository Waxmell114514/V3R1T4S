                                                reformulation based on generalised base-4 repunits. Any admissible exponent is even, say

1     Introduction
In a short 1979 article, Erdős conjectured that for k > 8 the number 2k cannot be represented
as a sum of distinct powers of 3, while recording the exceptional identity

                                  28 = 256 = 35 + 32 + 3 + 1.

Equivalently, the base-3 expansion of 2k contains no digit 2 only for the known exponents
k = 0, 2, 8 [1]. The weaker formulation asks merely whether there are finitely many such
exponents; this is the form emphasised in later dynamical and 3-adic work [4]. The problem is
still open in 2026 [10].
   The problem sits at an unusual interface. It is elementary to state, and any fixed exponent
is easy to test, yet an argument excluding all future exponents has resisted both computation
and known distribution theory. Gupta verified the conjecture through n = 4373 [2]; later work
substantially enlarged the computational range. In particular, Saye developed a recursive
trailing-digit construction and verified the conjecture for all n ≤ 2 · 345 ≈ 5.9 × 1021 [7].
Dimitrov and Howe proved that among powers of two expressible as sums of at most twenty-five
distinct powers of three, only the three known examples occur [9]. Lagarias developed real
and 3-adic dynamical formulations and connected the problem to Cantor-set intersections [4];
Abram and Lagarias subsequently studied finite intersections of multiplicative translates of the
3-adic Cantor set through finite automata and Hausdorff dimension [5]. Dupuy and Weirich
obtained average digit-equidistribution results whose non-averaged analogue would imply the
weak Erdős conjecture [6]. Aliyev has also analysed visible regularities in ternary digit blocks
of powers of two [8].
   The purpose of the present paper is different from another large computation and different
from a claim of resolution. It develops a coordinate system in which the local arithmetic of
the problem becomes exact and recursive. The presentation is deliberately self-contained: the
repunit identities, congruence expansions, residue permutations, canonical seeds and arithmetic
fibres are established from first principles before they are used. The resulting framework can
therefore be read, checked and reproduced from the present paper alone.
   The key observation is that the Erdős condition is naturally a base-4 repunit condition.
Once this bridge is made, the repunit block identity and its 3-adic truncations become directly
applicable. The resulting finite-depth maps are not merely convenient: they are permutations
of the residue rings Z/3m Z. This leads to canonical seeds, disjoint fibres and an exact lift law.
At the inverse-limit level, the finite residue maps extend to a natural 3-adic coordinate on
the exponent space. Taken together, these results completely characterise finite-depth suffix
admissibility in the repunit coordinate: every prescribed suffix has a unique exponent class, the
admissible classes form an exact binary lifting tree, and the compatible finite maps assemble
into an isometric 3-adic coordinate. They do not, however, resolve the ordinary-integer problem.
The unresolved Erdős conjecture asks which non-negative integers satisfy the digit restriction
throughout the complete ternary expansion.

1.1   Scope and novelty boundary
The contribution of this paper is the following synthesis:
• an exact reduction of the Erdős ternary-digit condition to digit omission for the generalised
  repunit Rs (4);

• a canonical seed-fibre stratification of exponent classes through the permutation

                                      Φm (s) = Rs (4)        (mod 3m );

• a repunit-coordinate formulation of the one-digit lifting law, in which each depth-m class
  has three lifts and exactly two preserve admissibility;
• an inverse-limit formulation in which the compatible maps Φm assemble into an isometric
  homeomorphism of Z3 conjugating x 7→ x + 1 with y 7→ 4y + 1.
    Taken together, these results provide a unified repunit-coordinate description of finite-depth
admissibility, canonical inverse fibres, and the associated 3-adic dynamics.
    Several ingredients used in establishing this framework are classical or already present in the
literature. Geometric-series identities, lifting-the-exponent arguments, multiplicative orders,
and the 3-adic Cantor set are not new. Most importantly, the recursive fact that there are
three exponent lifts at each new ternary digit and that exactly two avoid the digit 2 is already
embodied in Saye’s construction [7]. The contribution here is therefore not the discovery of
that recursion itself, but its integration into a repunit-coordinate framework that also exposes
the finite-level permutation structure, canonical seed fibres, and the compatible inverse-limit
conjugacy on Z3 .
    A finite-depth residue tree, no matter how deep, cannot by itself prove finiteness of ordinary
integer solutions, because the inverse-limit tree contains infinitely many 3-adic paths. Any
complete proof must therefore introduce a genuinely global mechanism beyond finite-depth
suffix admissibility.

3     The repunit reduction
The first theorem turns the ternary-digit problem into a repunit problem in a lossless way.
Theorem 3.1 (Parity and repunit equivalence). Suppose the ternary expansion of 2n contains
only 0 and 1. Then n is even. Writing n = 2s,

                                     22s = 4s = 1 + 3Rs (4),                                     (9)

and
                            22s ∈ Σfin
                                   3,{0,1}     ⇐⇒       Rs (4) ∈ Σfin
                                                                  3,{0,1} .                    (10)

Proof. If the ternary expansion of 2n omits 2, its least significant ternary digit cannot be 0
because 3 ∤ 2n ; it must therefore be 1. Hence 2n ≡ 1 (mod 3). Since 2 ≡ −1 (mod 3), this
forces n even, say n = 2s.
   The identity (9) is the definition of Rs (4). Since 4s ≡ 1 (mod 3), its least significant ternary
digit is 1. Subtracting 1 from 4s therefore causes no borrowing, and division by 3 simply shifts
the remaining ternary digits one place to the right. Thus the digits of Rs (4) are exactly the
ternary digits of 4s with the terminal 1 removed. This proves (10).

Corollary 3.2 (Equivalent exponent formulation). The sharp Erdős conjecture is equivalent
to
                         {s ∈ N0 : Rs (4) ∈ Σfin
                                             3,{0,1} } = {0, 1, 4}.

5.1     Relationship with Saye’s recursion
Saye proves an equivalent next-digit mechanism directly for powers of two. In his notation,
the smallest positive exponent period preserving k trailing ternary digits is uk = 2 · 3k−1 , and
the three lifts of an exponent through this period cycle the next ternary digit [7]. Under the
present reduction n = 2s, the exponent increment

                                                   2 · 3m

in n is exactly the increment 3m in s. Thus theorem 5.2 and corollary 5.3 are a repunit-
coordinate formulation of the same finite-depth arithmetic phenomenon.
   This equivalence is useful rather than redundant. Saye’s formulation is optimised for
computation on 2n ; the repunit formulation exposes an additional permutation structure,
canonical inverse fibres and a natural inverse-limit map on Z3 . In the present treatment the
recursion follows directly from the block identities proved in proposition 2.4.

6    The inverse-limit repunit coordinate

8.5   Reproducibility protocol
The finite computations require only exact integer arithmetic:
 1. compute Rs (4) = (4s − 1)/3 or, modulo 3m , use the recurrence Rs+1 ≡ 4Rs + 1;
 2. reduce Rs (4) modulo 3m and test whether every one of the m ternary digits lies in {0, 1};
 3. for canonical inversion, start with the unique seed at depth m and apply proposition 7.1;
 4. independently verify the relation to powers of two using 22s = 1 + 3Rs (4);
 5. for large depths, traverse only the two admissible children of each node rather than all 3m
    exponent classes.

nodes, consistent with Saye’s recursive approach [7].

10      Consequences of the framework
The preceding theorems give several immediate structural conclusions.

10.1     No finite suffix obstruction can resolve the conjecture
For every m ≥ 1, there are exactly 2m exponent classes modulo 3m for which the least significant
m ternary digits of Rs (4) all lie in {0, 1}. Moreover, by corollary 5.4, every admissible class
has exactly two admissible children at the next depth.
Consequently, the finite-depth admissibility tree never terminates. There is therefore no fixed
depth m at which all residue classes are eliminated, and no argument based solely on checking
a bounded number of trailing ternary digits can resolve the Erdős conjecture.
   What remains unresolved is a genuinely global question: whether an ordinary non-negative
integer s can satisfy the digit restriction throughout the complete ternary expansion of Rs (4),
rather than merely through any prescribed finite suffix.

Repunit Coordinates for the Erdős Ternary-Digit Problem                                M. A. Idowu


10.2    The exact local density is already determined
The finite-depth density is exactly (2/3)m . Therefore a heuristic treating successive admissibility
constraints as reducing the class count by a factor 2/3 happens to predict the correct count,
but not because the digits are independent. The exact reason is that Φm is a permutation and
the target set has cardinality 2m .

10.3    Sparse-support results impose a global threshold
If the sharp conjecture were false, a counterexample 2n would be a sum of distinct powers of 3.
Dimitrov and Howe prove that any counterexample beyond 1, 4, 256 would require at least
twenty-six non-zero ternary digits [9]. Since

                                        22s = 3Rs (4) + 1,

the terminal digit 1 of 22s is contributed by the added constant term, while the remaining
ternary digits are those of Rs (4), shifted one place to the left. Hence any new counterexample
would force Rs (4) to contain at least twenty-five 1-digits. This is a genuinely global restriction
that is invisible from any bounded ternary suffix.

11     Further Research
The framework is intended to sharpen the next mathematical question rather than obscure it.

11.1    Target A: an Archimedean escape theorem
A direct route would be to prove that there exists S0 such that every ordinary integer s > S0
has
                                  Φ(s) = Rs (4) ∈/ Σ3,{0,1} .
Such a theorem must use more than residue cylinders because every cylinder contains infinitely
many ordinary integers.
  Possible forms of a missing global estimate include:
• a lower bound on the number of ternary digit 2s in Rs (4) as a function of s;
• a carry-propagation theorem for the binomial expansion (7);
• a Diophantine approximation inequality incompatible with long or complete membership
  in Σ3,{0,1} ;
• an entropy or discrepancy statement strong enough to hold for each sufficiently large
  exponent rather than only on average.
   The last distinction is exactly the gap highlighted by Dupuy and Weirich: average equidis-
tribution is accessible, whereas a sufficiently strong non-averaged statement would imply the
Erdős conjecture [6].

coupling the exact local residue structure to genuinely global restrictions. We do not pursue
this direction further here.


12     Discussion
The repunit reduction changes the perspective in three ways.
   First, it removes the superficial asymmetry between a power of two and a sum of powers of
three. After the forced parity reduction, the problem concerns a single recurrence

                                 Rs+1 = 4Rs + 1,       R0 = 0,

with the requirement that every ternary digit of Rs lie in {0, 1}. The target set is the standard
ternary Cantor set; the orbit is an affine recurrence.
   Secondly, the map from exponent residues to repunit residues is as regular as possible: it is
a permutation at every 3-power modulus. This fact explains exactly why two of three lifts
survive at each digit. It also means there is no purely local congruence scarcity waiting to be
discovered; thus any proof must incorporate information not contained in finite-depth 3-adic
suffix admissibility, naturally pointing towards constraints involving ordinary size or other
global structure.
   Thirdly, the construction places computation and proof in different roles. Deep recursion
is excellent for verification and for discovering record admissible suffixes, as demonstrated at
extraordinary depth by Saye [7]. But the infinite binary tree proves that further depth alone
cannot become a proof. The research task is to find a theorem that cuts across the tree using
ordinary magnitude, sparse-support constraints, or a non-averaged distribution principle.
   This separation is valuable for a foundational programme. It identifies which statements
are exact, which are computational, and where the unsolved content begins.

13     Conclusion
The Erdős ternary-digit challenge can be reformulated exactly through the base-4 repunit

                                                   4s − 1
                                        Rs (4) =          .
                                                      3
Every admissible power of two has even exponent n = 2s, and digit omission for 22s is equivalent
to digit omission for Rs (4). The general block identity and finite p-adic expansion required for
this coordinate are proved explicitly in proposition 2.4 and theorem 2.5.
   At every depth m, the repunit residue map Φm (s) = Rs (4) mod 3m is a permutation.
Consequently, each target suffix has a unique canonical exponent seed modulo 3m , its full
source set is an arithmetic fibre, and the admissible suffixes determine exactly 2m classes. The
block lift
                             Rr+q3m (4) ≡ Rr (4) + q3m (mod 3m+1 )
explains the three-to-two branching law and recovers Saye’s trailing-digit recursion in repunit
coordinates.
The inverse-limit map Φ : Z3 → Z3 is an isometric homeomorphism and conjugates x 7→ x + 1
with y 7→ 4y + 1.
This is the central conceptual outcome: the local 3-adic problem is completely populated
and exactly recursive, whereas the unresolved content is global. A complete proof must
explain why, among ordinary non-negative integers s, only s = 0, 1, 4 produce repunits Rs (4)
whose full ternary expansions contain no digit 2. The framework therefore supplies a rigorous
finite-depth structure, a reproducible computational architecture, and a precise boundary for
future theorem development, but it does not resolve the Erdős conjecture.


A     A compact computational algorithm
A depth-first enumeration of admissible seeds may be stated as follows.

AdmissibleSeeds(M):
    states := {(0, 0)}    # (depth m, seed r modulo 3^m)
    leaves := {0}
    for m from 0 to M-1:
        next := empty set
        for r in leaves:
            d := floor(R_r(4) / 3^m) mod 3
            for t in {0,1,2}:
                if (d + t) mod 3 is in {0,1}:
                    next.add(r + t*3^m)
        leaves := next
    return leaves

Rr (4) mod 3m+1 , or to compute it by the binary power-and-sum recurrence given in section C.
The invariant after level m is that leaves contains exactly the 2m representatives of Am in
[0, 3m ).
