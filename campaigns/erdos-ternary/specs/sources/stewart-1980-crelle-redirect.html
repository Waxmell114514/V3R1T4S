We offer exciting and challenging programs leading to BMath, MMath and PhD degrees. We nurture a very active research environment and are intensely devoted to both ground-breaking research and excellent teaching.

Tuesday, November 24, 2026 10:00 am - 11:00 am EST (GMT -05:00)

Number Theory Seminar | Owen Sharpe | On the Selberg--Delange Method in Polynomial Rings over Finite Fields

Owen Sharpe (University of Waterloo)

On the Selberg--Delange Method in Polynomial Rings over Finite Fields

Let \(q\) be a prime power and let \(\mathbb{F}_q[t]\) be the polynomial ring over the finite field of order \(q\).

For each nonzero \(F \in \mathbb{F}_q[t]\), let \(\Omega(F)\) denote the number of monic irreducible factors of \(F\), counted with multiplicity.

Using the Selberg--Delange method, we obtain asymptotic expansions for the sum of \(z^{\Omega(F)}\) over all monic polynomials \(F\) of degree \(n\) and for the number of such polynomials satisfying \(\Omega(F)=k\).

Our method differs from Delange's treatment of the integer case because of an additional dependence on \(n\) in the Hankel integral.

We overcome this obstacle in the polynomial case by expanding the integral itself into an asymptotic series involving the reciprocal Gamma function.

These expansions improve the precision of previous results of Warlimont and Datta.

This is joint work with Sourabhashis Das, Wentang Kuo, and Yu-Ru Liu.

Geometry and Topology Seminar | Jacek Rzemieniecki | Some questions and some answers about Fueter maps

Jacek Rzemieniecki (HU Berlin)

Some questions and some answers about Fueter maps

Fueter maps are a quaternionic generalization of holomorphic maps. They appear naturally in a host of geometric contexts ranging from gauge theory and manifolds with exceptional holonomy to proposals for categorifying invariants arising in symplectic geometry. A fundamental difficulty in all these directions is the failure of compactness.

The general compactness theory allows sequences of Fueter maps to bubble along a codimension-two subset. I will discuss recent progress which suggests that such bubbling is in fact highly nongeneric for Hamiltonian-perturbed Fueter equations. More precisely, under natural assumptions, I will explain why the Hamiltonians for which bubbling can occur form an exceptional set of infinite codimension. I will also discuss new constructions of Fueter maps and some related open problems.

Student Number Theory Seminar | Jarry Gu | Mahler Measure Take Two : in Several Variables and the Regulator Method

Jarry Gu (University of Waterloo)

Mahler Measure Take Two : in Several Variables and the Regulator Method

The Mahler measure of a polynomial, introduced in Julius' previous talk in connexion with Lehmer's conjecture, admits a natural extension to polynomials in several variables. We will explain how Jensen's formula leads to this generalisation and briefly discuss the relation between multivariate and one-variable Mahler measures given by the Boyd–Lawton theorem. We will study the example of m(1+x+x^2+y), first using ordinary integration, and then approaching the same computation through the theory of regulators. This will illustrate how regulator forms on algebraic curves can transform Mahler measure computations into evaluations involving special functions
